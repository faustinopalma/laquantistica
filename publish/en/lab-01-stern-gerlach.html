      Vacuum

      Working pressure
      The atoms must cross the apparatus without hitting the residual gas: the mean free path must be far longer than the beam.

      Source

      Crucible temperature
      Melting is not enough (962 °C): what counts is the vapour pressure. But too high and the atoms collide in the hole, so the beam is no longer effusive.
      Crucible hole ⌀
      Do not confuse two different vacuums: inside the crucible the gas is silver vapour at a few pascal (λ ≈ 1 mm), outside it is the pump's residual gas, ten thousand times thinner (λ ≈ 10 m). The collisions that matter all happen within one hole diameter, because just outside the beam density falls as 1/r².

      Collimation

      Slit width
      Narrow = sharp but weak beam. Wide = intense but blurred: the trace broadens until it swallows the splitting.
      Slit separation

      Magnet (coaxial)

      Inner (edge) radius
      The sharp inner pole: the smaller it is, the more the field — and the gradient — crowd around it. In the thesis design it is 4 mm.
      Outer (groove) radius
      The concave outer pole, concentric with the inner one (same centre, as in the section drawing). The radial gap is the difference of the two radii. In the design it is 26 mm.
      Eccentricity (centres offset)
      At 0% the two circles are concentric (as in the design drawing). Increasing it shifts the inner circle to one side: there the gap narrows and the field concentrates, as in the classic offset edge-and-groove magnet.
      Beam position in the gap
      Where the beam runs between the edge (0%) and the groove (100%). Near the edge the gradient is very strong but the room is little: the beam risks hitting the pole. The field goes as 1/r, the gradient as 1/r².
